When people first encounter Traditional Chinese Medicine food therapy or acupressure online, they almost always run into the same frustration: the exact same cup of ginger tea or bowl of raw greens makes one person feel clear-headed and leaves another bloated, wired, or exhausted.

In clinical Chinese medicine, that difference is mapped through **body constitution** (*tizhi*, 體質) — standardized in 2009 by Professor Wang Qi and the China Association of Chinese Medicine (CACM) into [the nine TCM body constitutions](https://www.identifytcm.com/constitution): one Balanced baseline (*Pinghe*) and eight specific tendencies such as Qi Deficiency, Yang Deficiency, Yin Deficiency, Phlegm-Dampness, and Qi Stagnation.

Over the past eight months (`2026-03-17` to `2026-11-18`), visitors from **164 countries and regions** completed **21,242** 29-question constitution assessments on IdentifyTCM (representing 14,104 unique respondents). We recently opened up the anonymized aggregate numbers on our [live TCM constitution statistics page](https://www.identifytcm.com/constitution-statistics). Looking at the dataset side-by-side with classical community surveys reveals three striking patterns about modern everyday wellbeing.

### 1. Qi Stagnation (26.4%) Tops Every Single Adult Age Band

Across all 21,242 completed assessments, **[Qi Stagnation](https://www.identifytcm.com/constitution/qi-stagnation) (*Qiyu*, 氣鬱質)** is the #1 primary constitution by a wide margin:

1. **Qi Stagnation (`qiyu`):** 26.4% (5,615 assessments)
2. **Qi Deficiency (`qixu`):** 16.9% (3,584)
3. **Special / Inherited Sensitivity (`tebing`):** 16.2% (3,445)
4. **Phlegm-Dampness (`tanshi`):** 11.2% (2,373)
5. **Yang Deficiency (`yanxu`):** 9.4% (1,995)
6. **Yin Deficiency (`yinxu`):** 7.8% (1,651)
7. **Balanced (`pinghe`):** 6.2% (1,311)
8. **Damp-Heat (`shire`):** 3.9% (822)
9. **Blood Stasis (`xueyu`):** 2.1% (446)

In classical TCM, Qi Stagnation reflects constrained Liver-channel flow from chronic mental load, screen-bound posture, and emotional tension — showing up as frequent sighing, rib-side or jaw tightness, pre-period tension, or waking between 1 AM and 3 AM. In our dataset, it peaks at **36.7%** in the `30–39` working-and-caregiving decade (`n = 2,084`), and still remains #1 even after age 60 (`23.0%`, `n = 9,584`).

### 2. Why Only 6.2% Score "Balanced" Online (vs. 25%–32% in General Surveys)

If you read large epidemiological papers conducted in Chinese community cohorts, the Balanced (*Pinghe*) constitution typically accounts for roughly a quarter to a third of the general population. Why is it only **6.2% (~1 in 16)** here — and just **2.4% to 3.1%** among adults under 40?

Methodological honesty matters: an online wellness assessment is a **self-selected health-seeking sample**, not a random street census. People rarely take a three-minute body-type quiz when they sleep eight effortless hours and wake up full of energy; they take it when they are trying to make sense of afternoon crashes, cold feet, or restless nights. Among people actively looking for daily self-care answers, roughly 15 out of 16 already carry at least one clear constitutional tilt worth respecting.

### 3. Geography & Climate Leave Visible Fingerprints

Even in an online sample, local environment shows up in the rankings. While Qi Stagnation ranks #1 across North America and Europe (United States `n = 8,075`, UK `n = 1,242`, Canada `n = 1,197`, Germany `n = 457`), two humid Asian hubs — **Taiwan (`n = 350`)** and **Singapore (`n = 287`)** — rank **Phlegm-Dampness** #1, while **Australia (`n = 769`)**, famous for intense seasonal pollen counts, ranks **Special / Sensitive Constitution** #1.
