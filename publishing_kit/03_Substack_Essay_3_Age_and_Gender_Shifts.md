- **Subtitle:** Age and gender trends across five decades of adulthood — and why the habits that worked at 28 stop working at 52.

Classical Chinese medicine texts — starting with the first chapter of the *Huangdi Neijing Suwen* — describe human physiology not as a static trait, but as a multi-decade arc. What happens when we test that classical intuition against **21,242 modern assessments** spanning ages 18 to 80+?

Stratifying our [open TCM constitution dataset](https://www.identifytcm.com/constitution-statistics) across five adult age bands (`18–29`, `30–39`, `40–49`, `50–59`, and `60+`) and by gender (`15,593` women and `5,508` men) highlights three clear inflection points.

### 1. Ages 30–39: The Peak of Qi Stagnation (36.7%)

Across every age group, Qi Stagnation ranks #1 — but it reaches its sharpest peak in the **30–39 band (36.7%, n = 2,084)**, alongside 34.7% in ages 18–29. These are the high-output career and caregiving years, when sleep debt and prolonged sitting constrain circulation before deeper deficiency sets in.

### 2. Ages 40–49: The Midlife Phlegm-Dampness Inflection (14.6%)

In the `40–49` cohort (`n = 2,664`), **Phlegm-Dampness** jumps from 9.5% to **14.6%**, overtaking Qi Deficiency to become the #2 primary pattern. Cold smoothies, late dinners, and desk work that the body shrugged off at 25 begin showing up as morning heaviness and slower fluid metabolism. Notably, **Damp-Heat** also skews **2.1× higher in men (6.3%) than in women (3.0%)**.

### 3. Ages 50 to 60+: Yin Deficiency More Than Doubles (4.0% → 9.6%)

Perhaps the clearest classical confirmation appears after 50 (`n = 5,846` in 50–59; `n = 9,584` in 60+):

- **[Yin Deficiency](https://www.identifytcm.com/constitution/yin-deficiency) rises 2.4×**, climbing from 4.0% in ages 40–49 to **7.1%** in 50–59 and **9.6%** in 60+. In TCM, as cooling, moistening Yin and Liver Blood naturally decline, people notice lighter sleep, night warmth, dry eyes, or subtle surface clues such as [vertical ridges on the fingernails](https://www.identifytcm.com/learn/what-do-my-nails-say-about-my-health). For women navigating hot flashes and sleep changes, understanding [how TCM approaches menopause as a Yin transition](https://www.identifytcm.com/learn/tcm-for-menopause) shifts the focus from "fighting heat with ice" toward gentle moistening and earlier rest.
- **Special / Sensitive Constitution (`tebing`) climbs steadily with age**, rising from 11.2% in ages 18–29 to **18.7%** in the 60+ cohort, becoming the #2 pattern among older adults.
