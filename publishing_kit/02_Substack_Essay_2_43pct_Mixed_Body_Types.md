One of the most common questions I hear from viewers and readers is: *"I took a body-type checklist and seemed to match two opposite descriptions at the same time — did I answer it wrong?"*

According to our dataset of 21,234 scored assessments on IdentifyTCM, you didn't answer it wrong at all. In **43.2% (9,171 assessments)**, a person's top two biased constitutions sit within **5 points or fewer** of each other on a 100-point scale. Pure textbook single-type bodies are the exception; [mixed TCM body types](https://www.identifytcm.com/learn/mixed-tcm-body-types-why-you-fit-more-than-one) are everyday reality.

### The Top 5 Real-World Combinations (Out of 9,171 Near-Ties)

When we ranked every unordered pair whose top two biased scores sat within 5 points, five combinations accounted for nearly half (45.3%) of all dual profiles:

1. **Qi Deficiency + Qi Stagnation (13.2% of near-ties, n = 1,211):** The classic modern "depleted yet tense" pattern — your battery runs low by mid-afternoon, yet tight ribs, shallow breathing, or racing thoughts keep you from resting deeply.
2. **Qi Stagnation + Yin Deficiency (8.7%, n = 796):** Long-term tension gradually drying out cooling reserves — light sleep, dry eyes after screen work, and warm palms at night.
3. **Qi Stagnation + Phlegm-Dampness (8.3%, n = 758):** Stress slowing digestive movement, leading to post-meal heaviness, stress bloating, and morning brain fog.
4. **Damp-Heat + Phlegm-Dampness (7.8%, n = 717):** Sluggish fluid metabolism paired with internal heat.
5. **Qi Stagnation + Yang Deficiency (7.3%, n = 667):** Often experienced as icy hands and feet under stress, raising the classic question of [why you feel cold all the time](https://www.identifytcm.com/learn/why-am-i-always-cold) — whether your internal heater is genuinely low or warmth is simply trapped in the core ([Qi Deficiency vs. Yang Deficiency](https://www.identifytcm.com/learn/always-cold-qi-deficiency-vs-yang-deficiency)).

### Why Sequencing Beats Piling On

Mixed patterns explain why generic wellness trends backfire so often:

- **When Qi Deficiency meets Dampness (`n = 532` near-ties):** Taking rich, heavy tonics on top of sluggish digestion clogs the middle burner further. Clinical sequencing clears and lightens first — using gentle warm cooked meals and [practical ways to clear dampness](https://www.identifytcm.com/learn/how-to-get-rid-of-dampness) — before building Qi.
- **When Qi Stagnation meets Deficiency (`n = 1,211` near-ties):** Strongly drying herbs can deplete Yin and Blood, while heavy tonics feel suffocating if stuck Qi isn't gently moved first with walking, breathwork, or light acupressure.
